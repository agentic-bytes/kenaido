**In short:** kenaido's agents are AI models playing roles. They are not people,
and no group of them is a real Scrum Team. Check everything they produce, keep a
person accountable for every decision, and use kenaido at your own risk. This
file is information, not legal advice.

We wrote this so nobody — least of all someone new to AI or Scrum — is led to believe something about kenaido that is not true.

## 1. The agents are AI models, not people

- A kenaido "agent" is an AI model, run by the coding agent tool you chose,
  given a role description to follow. It is not a person, an employee, or a
  professional, and has no experience or responsibility of its own.
- Each agent acts only on the limited instructions in its role definition,
  which may not cover every aspect of the role. It never becomes the human
  professional the role is named after.
- Its output comes from a statistical model: the same request can give different
  answers, and results change with the model, its version, its settings, and the
  tool running it. A result good once may not be good next time.
- **Because of what they are, no group of agents can ever match a real Scrum Team
  of people.** No rule, role, or setting in kenaido changes that.
- Agents can be wrong while sounding sure, including inventing facts, sources,
  or rules that do not exist.
- Agents are instructed to say they are AI when asked, correct anyone who
  takes them for people, and present their work as something to check — like
  any instruction, they may fail to follow it.
- Where a role asks an agent to work "at expert level", that is an instruction the
  agent is given, not a quality it is certain to reach. A test of a role, by
  anyone, says nothing certain about how it behaves on your model.

## 2. The frameworks are simulated, and the role names are a map

- Scrum, the multi-team scaling framework, and every other framework,
  methodology, or practice kenaido draws on are **simulated** for agents: kenaido
  gives the agents, as closely as it can, the same guidance those guides give
  people in the same roles. Agents following guidance are not a team of people
  practicing the framework.
- Role names such as Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developer, Legal Counsel, or
  Security Expert are used only so you know what to expect of each agent. An
  agent with a role's name does not hold that role: kenaido's rules give every
  accountability to a person, and making sure a person holds it is up to you.

## 3. How kenaido is meant to be used, and what stays yours

These points are not conditions of kenaido's licenses. They say how kenaido is
meant to be used, and which things only you can do.

- **What kenaido is for.** kenaido is meant to help a team run its software work
  with coding agents inside a simulated Scrum life cycle, with people deciding.
  It is **not** meant for high-risk uses, for example decisions about people's
  jobs, credit, health, safety, or access to essential services.
- **Check every result** before you use it: code, documents, analyses,
  decisions, and anything that affects people.
- **Keep a person accountable** for every decision. kenaido's rules say so; they
  cannot make it happen for you.
- **Rules guide the agents; they do not bind them.** An agent reads kenaido's
  rules and may still act against them. Only checks outside the agent, such as
  your tool's permission settings, git hooks you turn on, pipeline checks, and a
  person's approval, can stop an action, and only as far as they are set up and
  working.
- **Say when AI did the work.** kenaido asks that people be told when they are
  dealing with an AI agent, and that AI-written work be marked as such where it
  reaches others. Some laws set duties of this kind, for example Article 50 of
  the EU AI Act. Whether any applies to you depends on your role and where you
  work; kenaido cannot tell you. Ask a qualified professional.
- **No agent is a licensed professional.** No agent is a lawyer, accountant,
  auditor, or other licensed professional, whatever its role is called, and
  nothing it produces is legal, tax, financial, medical, security, or other
  professional advice. Where your work needs such advice, ask a qualified person.
- **Your tools, your terms.** kenaido supplies no AI model and no agent tool. The
  tool and the model provider you use set their own terms, limits, and handling
  of your data, and following them is up to you.
- **What you build is yours.** If you build, publish, or ship anything with
  kenaido's help, the duties that come with it, under the law and under anyone
  else's terms, are yours. Using kenaido does not make you compliant with any
  law or standard, and kenaido claims no such thing.
