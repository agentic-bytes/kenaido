## What it is

Two or three sentences, in plain words, that let a reader who has never heard of it use it today (`COMM-3`). A diagram or a small table if it carries the idea better than prose (`COMM-7`). If the tool comes from a published method, say so in your own words, never copy its text, and never present a trademarked name as ours; label anything about its origin you could not verify (`COMM-4`, `ANLY-2`).

## When to use it

The shapes of question it fits. Be concrete: "a request that could mean several things" rather than "unclear situations".

## When not to use it

**Required, and the most useful section in the file.** Where the tool misleads, costs more than it gives, or is the wrong shape for the question — and what to reach for instead. A tool without this section becomes ceremony.

## How to run it

Numbered steps, each one an action. Say what to do with the output and where it is recorded (`ANLY-5`). If the tool runs in passes, say how a later pass differs from the first, and state where it stops — the general stopping rules are in [`README.md`](README.md) (`ANLY-10`).

## What it produces

The artifact, in one or two lines, and which part of it is the real output. Often that is the list of unknowns, not the filled-in structure.

## Common mistakes

The three or four ways it goes wrong in practice, each written so a reviewer can spot it.

## Rules applied
